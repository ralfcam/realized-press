## Findings

### Query 1 — Davis & Zhong (2017) Biology of Active Forgetting
> **Davis, R. L., and Zhong, Y.** "The Biology of Forgetting—A Perspective." *Neuron* 95, no. 3 (2017): 490–503. DOI: 10.1016/j.neuron.2017.05.039.
Davis and Zhong showed that forgetting is not merely passive metabolic decay or interference. It is an active biological program regulated by specific dopamine receptors and small G-protein rac1 cascades that disassemble the actin cytoskeleton of dendritic spines.

### Query 2 — Richards & Frankland (2017) Adaptive Transience
> **Richards, B. A., and Frankland, P. W.** "The Persistence and Transience of Memory." *Neuron* 94, no. 6 (2017): 1071–1084. DOI: 10.1016/j.neuron.2017.04.037.
Richards and Frankland argued that the neurobiological goal of memory is not high-fidelity record-keeping, but intelligent decision-making in noisy, dynamic environments. Forgetting promotes behavioral flexibility and prevents overfitting.
