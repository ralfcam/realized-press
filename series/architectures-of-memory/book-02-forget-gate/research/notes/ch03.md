## Findings

### Query 1 & 3 — The 1997 Architecture and Saturation
The original 1997 LSTM introduced input gates and output gates to shield the CEC from irrelevant incoming noise and prevent the network from emitting premature outputs. However, it had no mechanism to discard stored information. In continuous sequential tasks lacking explicit reset signals at sequence boundaries, the internal cell state grew monotonically, saturating output non-linearities and degrading representational capacity.

### Query 2 — Greff et al. (2017) Search Space Odyssey
> **Greff, K., Srivastava, R. K., Koutník, J., Steunebrink, B. R., and Schmidhuber, J.** "LSTM: A Search Space Odyssey." *IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems* 28, no. 10 (2017): 2222–2232. DOI: 10.1109/TNNLS.2016.2582924.
Greff et al. conducted an exhaustive ablation study across 5,400 runs on speech, handwriting, and music datasets. They proved that the forget gate is the single most critical component of the LSTM; removing it significantly degrades performance across all domains.

### Query 4 — Locke's Memory Criterion
John Locke (*An Essay Concerning Human Understanding*, 1689, Book II, Chapter XXVII) posited that personal identity extends as far as consciousness and memory can reach backwards. An un-gated memory cell mirrors Locke's criterion taken to an unbounded extreme: an archive incapable of forgetting becomes an undifferentiated mass.
