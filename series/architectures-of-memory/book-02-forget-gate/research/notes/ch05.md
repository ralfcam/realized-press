## Findings

### Query 1 & 2 — Parallel Parameterized Gates
The three gates of an LSTM—intake ($i_t$), forget ($f_t$), and output ($o_t$)—are not heuristic logical switches. Each gate is an independent affine transformation followed by a sigmoid non-linearity:
$$\mathbf{i}_t = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_i \mathbf{x}_t + \mathbf{U}_i \mathbf{h}_{t-1} + \mathbf{b}_i)$$
$$\mathbf{f}_t = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_f \mathbf{x}_t + \mathbf{U}_f \mathbf{h}_{t-1} + \mathbf{b}_f)$$
$$\mathbf{o}_t = \sigma(\mathbf{W}_o \mathbf{x}_t + \mathbf{U}_o \mathbf{h}_{t-1} + \mathbf{b}_o)$$
Because the outputs lie in the open interval $(0, 1)$, gating acts as a differentiable, soft attentional filter.

### Query 3 — Graves and Schmidhuber (2005)
> **Graves, A., and Schmidhuber, J.** "Framewise phoneme classification with bidirectional LSTM and other neural network architectures." *Neural Networks* 18, no. 5–6 (2005): 602–610. DOI: 10.1016/j.neunet.2005.06.042.
Graves demonstrated that bidirectional LSTMs with learned continuous gates outperformed standard recurrent networks and HMMs on the TIMIT speech corpus.
