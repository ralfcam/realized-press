## Findings

### Query 1 — Hochreiter's 1991 Master's Thesis
Sepp Hochreiter completed his master's thesis (Diplomarbeit) in 1991 at the Technische Universität München under the supervision of Wilfried Brauer. The thesis is titled:
> **Hochreiter, S.** *Untersuchungen zu dynamischen neuronalen Netzen*. Diplomarbeit, Institut für Informatik, Technische Universität München (1991).

Hochreiter mathematically proved that error gradients flowing backwards through time in recurrent neural networks undergo exponential decay or explosion whenever the spectral radius of the transition matrix deviates from unity.

### Query 2 — IDSIA Collaboration in Lugano
Jürgen Schmidhuber established his research group at the Dalle Molle Institute for Artificial Intelligence (IDSIA) in Lugano, Switzerland. Hochreiter joined IDSIA in 1995, where they developed the Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) architecture to circumvent exponential gradient decay.

### Query 3 — Bengio et al. (1994) Independent Confirmation
Yoshua Bengio, Patrice Simard, and Paolo Frasconi independently published their formal analysis in 1994:
> **Bengio, Y., Simard, P., and Frasconi, P.** "Learning long-term dependencies with gradient descent is difficult." *IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks* 5, no. 2 (1994): 157–166.

They proved that storing a single bit of information robustly in an attractor network requires a contracting dynamical system, which mathematically forces the backpropagated error gradient to decay to zero.

### Query 4 — Canonical 1997 Publication
The seminal LSTM paper was published in *Neural Computation* in 1997:
> **Hochreiter, S., and Schmidhuber, J.** "Long Short-Term Memory." *Neural Computation* 9, no. 8 (1997): 1735–1780. DOI: 10.1162/neco.1997.9.8.1735.
