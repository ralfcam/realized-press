Depreciation of Organizational Knowledge in Industrial Production (Argote, Beckman & Epple 1990)
Empirical econometric analysis of manufacturing production curves shows organizational knowledge depreciates rapidly over interruptions, proving that institutional memory behaves as a perishable stock rather than a permanent asset. Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) vs. LSTM: Coupled Gating Mechanisms and Architectural Trade-offs (Cho et al. 2014)
GRU simplifies LSTM by coupling the forget and input mechanisms into a single update gate and eliminating the dedicated cell state, achieving comparable sequence modeling performance with 25% fewer parameters.		 HIGH gru, lstm, gating-mechanisms, deep-learning, rnn HTML PDF
Active Forgetting via Dedicated Molecular Signaling Pathways (Davis & Zhong 2017)
Review of neurobiological evidence showing that forgetting is not passive decay but an active cellular process governed by specific signaling pathways (including Rac1 and dopamine signaling) required for behavioral flexibility.		 HIGH active-forgetting, molecular-neuroscience, rac1, dopamine, cognitive-flexibility, transience HTML PDF
Adaptive Forget Gates in Long Short-Term Memory Networks (Gers, Schmidhuber & Cummins 2000)
Adding a multiplicative forget gate to the LSTM constant error carousel enables recurrent networks to reset internal state dynamically, solving continual time-series tasks where standard LSTM fails.		 HIGH lstm, forget-gate, continual-learning HTML PDF
Peephole Connections in Long Short-Term Memory Networks (Gers & Schmidhuber 2000)
Direct recurrent connections from internal cell states to gating units enable LSTMs to measure interval durations and count events precisely without requiring output activation.		 HIGH lstm, peephole-connections, interval-timing, count-state HTML PDF
LSTM Architectural Component Ablation: A Search Space Odyssey (Greff et al. 2017)
Large-scale empirical evaluation of eight LSTM variants across 5,400 experimental runs on speech recognition, handwriting recognition, and polyphonic music modeling, demonstrating that none of the popular variants significantly outperform the standard vanilla LSTM, and that the forget gate is the single most crucial component.		 HIGH lstm, ablation-study, deep-learning, forget-gate, recurrent-neural-networks, empirical-benchmark HTML PDF
The Constant Error Carousel in Long Short-Term Memory (Hochreiter & Schmidhuber 1997)
Linear additive recurrent self-connections with unit weight enforce constant error flow, circumventing the exponential vanishing and exploding gradients of multiplicative recurrent backpropagation.		 HIGH lstm, constant-error-carousel, vanishing-gradient, recurrent-neural-networks HTML PDF
The Dynamics of Organizational Forgetting: Accidental vs. Intentional Modes (de Holan & Phillips 2004)
A 4-quadrant taxonomy differentiates accidental memory loss (decay and failure to capture) from purposeful organizational forgetting (unlearning and managed forgetting), framing forgetting as an active governance capability.		 MODERATE organizational-memory, unlearning, managed-forgetting, institutional-amnesia HTML PDF
Overcoming Catastrophic Forgetting via Elastic Weight Consolidation (Kirkpatrick et al. 2017)
Demonstrated that neural networks can retain prior task competencies during sequential learning by penalizing parameter shifts proportionally to their Fisher information matrix importance.		 HIGH catastrophic-forgetting, continual-learning, elastic-weight-consolidation, deep-learning, neuroscience HTML PDF
Organizational Learning, Routines, and Competency Traps (Levitt & March 1988)
Barbara Levitt and James G. March established that organizations learn by encoding historical inferences into enduring routines, showing how competency traps and superstitious learning cause severe institutional lock-in unless obsolete routines are systematically pruned.		 MODERATE organizational-learning, routines, competency-trap, superstitious-learning, march HTML PDF
Adaptive Forgetting as Generalization in Biological Memory (Richards & Frankland 2017)
Neurobiological evidence demonstrates that memory transience is an active, regulated computational process that optimizes decision-making by eliminating outdated details and preventing overfitting to past environments, resolving the Borges Funes the Memorious memory curse.		 HIGH neuroscience, adaptive-forgetting, generalization, transience-of-memory, borges-funes, memory-curse HTML PDF
History Compression and Hierarchical Recurrent Chunkers (Schmidhuber 1992)
Jürgen Schmidhuber formulated the principle of neural history compression, demonstrating that recurrent hierarchies can bridge extended temporal delays by collapsing predictable sequence segments into compressed, slower-ticking representations.
