# Working Memory and Cognitive Shifting in Infancy and Toddlerhood

> Domain: cognition / executive function
> Last updated: 2026-10-21
> Status: Source-audited v0.4 domain card (CH11). Distinguishes normative developmental trajectories of working memory and attentional switching from popular cognitive-training claims.

## What We Know

Executive function is not a unitary skill that arrives all at once. Working memory (the ability to hold mental representations active across a temporal delay to guide subsequent action) and cognitive shifting (the capacity to disengage from an established rule set and switch attention to a new dimension) follow marked developmental trajectories across the first three years of life. Performance on delayed search tasks (such as Piaget's A-not-B paradigm) improves progressively between 7.5 and 12 months, reflecting functional maturation of the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex. Dimensional task switching advances substantially between ages 3 and 4, transitioning from perseverative rule maintenance to flexible set-shifting.

## How Confident We Are

**Grade**: Moderate-to-High for the normative developmental progression of working-memory delay capacity in infancy and dimensional attentional shifting in toddlerhood. **Low** that performance on individual infant search or sorting tasks provides a reliable long-term individual forecast of adult intelligence, or that infant working memory can be accelerated through specialized training products.

## Evidence and Causal Assessment

Diamond (1985), *Child Development*, longitudinally tracked infants aged 6 to 12 months on the A-not-B task with varying delay intervals (0 to 10 seconds). The duration of delay infants could tolerate before committing the perseverative reaching error increased systematically at roughly 2 to 3 seconds per month between 7.5 and 12 months. Successful search required the co-maturation of two interrelated capacities: working memory to maintain the representation of the hidden object's new location (B) across the delay, and inhibitory control to suppress the conditioned motor habit of reaching to the previously rewarded location (A).

Zelazo (2006), *Nature Protocols*, systematized the Dimensional Change Card Sort (DCCS) paradigm, demonstrating that 3-year-old children can readily sort cards by an initial dimension (e.g., color) and can verbally state the post-switch rule (e.g., sort by shape), but nevertheless perseverate by continuing to sort by the original dimension. By age 4 to 5, children reliably shift between dimensions, reflecting the development of higher-order hierarchical rule representation (the Cognitive Complexity and Control theory).

## Effect Size and Persistence

Normative developmental transitions exhibit large age-related effects (*d* > 0.8 between 7 and 12 months on A-not-B delay duration, and between age 3 and age 4 on post-switch DCCS sorting). However, individual rank-order stability across early childhood is modest (*r* ~ 0.30–0.40); infant working-memory metrics share substantial variance with general processing speed and receptive vocabulary, rather than acting as an isolated, stable executive trait.

## Bias, Heterogeneity, and Confounding

GI attenuation: **untested-in-GI** (normative developmental trajectory replicated across international cohorts; genetic and environmental contributions to early infant delay capacity are largely shared with general cognitive ability and linguistic comprehension).

Task-specific confounding is pronounced: an infant's failure on delayed reaching can arise from transient motor clumsiness, poor spatial acuity, fussiness, or examiner distraction rather than cognitive incapacity. In toddler card sorts, failure to switch often reflects attentional capture by salient perceptual features rather than a general absence of memory.

## What Remains Uncertain

How rapidly individual differences in early infant working-memory maintenance stabilize across the transition to formal schooling; and the exact degree to which parental scaffolding (talking through rule changes) causally accelerates prefrontal functional connectivity compared to intrinsic biological maturation.

## What This Does Not Mean

Does not mean an 8-month-old who searches at location A lacks memory or object permanence. Does not mean a 3-year-old who perseverates on the card sort is stubborn or cognitively delayed. Does not support commercial claims that infant flashcards, digital apps, or structured drill exercises will build a superior working memory.

## What Action Is Reasonably Justified

Caregivers should provide rich everyday opportunities for predictable, playful engagement that exercises working memory and cognitive flexibility—such as peekaboo, hide-and-seek, simple sorting during cleanup, and songs with alternating actions—while recognizing that perseveration and short-term forgetting are normal signatures of developing prefrontal circuits.
