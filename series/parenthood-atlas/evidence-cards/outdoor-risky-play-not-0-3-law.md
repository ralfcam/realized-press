# Risky Outdoor Play Is Not a 0–3 Developmental Law

> Domain: play / outdoor
> Last updated: 2026-09-16
> Status: Source-audited first pass (CH12 outdoor/risky remainder). Symbolic-play, Lillard, exploration, and peer cards are neighbors, not rewritten.

## What We Know

Brussoni and colleagues (2015) systematically reviewed studies of risky outdoor play and health. The literature, largely school-age, reports associations with physical activity and some social and injury-pattern outcomes. It does not isolate risky play as a unique cause of later intelligence, self-control, or school success.

## How Confident We Are

**Grade**: Moderate that, in this mostly older-child literature, risky outdoor play is associated with some health-related outcomes rather than being uniformly harmful. **Low-to-Moderate** as a 0–3 developmental law. **Low** as a toddler efficacy package or a unique-cause educational claim.

## Evidence and Causal Assessment

Systematic review, not an RCT of 0–3 outdoor play. Age bands, definitions of “risky,” and outcomes vary. Adjacent: `symbolic-pretend-play-emergence.md` and `pretend-play-educational-claims.md` already refuse play-as-curriculum. Exploration (`exploration-as-sampling.md`) is a different identification. Screens are not re-theorized as outdoor’s opposite.

## Effect Size and Persistence

No portable *d* for “risky play” in toddlers. Injury findings are mixed and definition-dependent. Persistence into later competence is largely untested in this atlas’s window.

## Bias, Heterogeneity, and Confounding

GI attenuation: **untested-in-GI** (no twin or sibling designs for 0–3 outdoor play; observational associations reflect family lifestyle selection).

School-age samples dominate. Families who allow more outdoor risk also differ in resources, neighborhoods, and supervision. Injury surveillance and play observation are not the same design.

## What Remains Uncertain

What, if anything, transfers from toddler stepping-and-climbing to the school-age risky-play literature; how much supervision, surface, and setting change the claim.

## What This Does Not Mean

Outdoor play is not worthless. It is not a registered 0–3 intelligence or character program. Removing all challenge is not shown to be required, and unsupervised hazard is not a developmental necessity.

## What Action Is Reasonably Justified

Do not import school-age reviews as toddler laws. Safety and opportunity are both live Art. A risky-play product does not follow.
