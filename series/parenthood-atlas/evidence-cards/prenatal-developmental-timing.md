# Prenatal Developmental Timing Is Process-Specific

> Domain: pregnancy
> Last updated: 2026-09-15
> Status: Source-audited first pass (CH02-C01)

## What We Know

Susceptibility to a prenatal exposure varies with developmental stage, dose, and the process underway. Organogenesis is more vulnerable to structural malformation than later fetal growth; the same agent is not one “prenatal effect.”

## How Confident We Are

**Grade**: Moderate for the process-specific timing principle in human teratology; **High** that there is no single nine-month window. Direct timed human experiments are limited; the principle is carried by experimental teratology plus clinical pattern.

## Evidence and Causal Assessment

Wilson’s principles, still treated as foundational in later reviews (Ujházy et al. 2012), state that teratogenic susceptibility varies with developmental stage, that access to embryonic tissues matters, and that manifestations increase with dose from no-effect to lethal. Human application rests on clinical timing (e.g. organogenesis vs later growth/functional injury), not on randomized timed exposures.

## Effect Size and Persistence

There is no universal effect size. The unit is exposure × window × process × outcome. Persistence must be measured for each pairing; a sensitive period is not an inevitable-injury window.

## Bias, Heterogeneity, and Confounding

GI attenuation: **n/a** (embryological organogenesis windows, not an observational population phenotype association).

Model-system translation; sparse direct human observation of early embryos; dating error; selection into ART and clinical series.

## What Remains Uncertain

How far mild, everyday exposures map onto the same timing rules as classic teratogens; human gastrulation remains thinly observed.

## What This Does Not Mean

Does not mean one missed week “ruins” development, or that every prenatal difference is a malformation.

## What Action Is Reasonably Justified

Public-health timing for known teratogens and periconceptional folate is justified. Treating pregnancy as a single optimization window is not.
