# Early Physical Reasoning and Object Permanence: Looking vs. Manual Search

> Domain: early learning, object permanence, infant cognition
> Last updated: 2026-10-29
> Status: OKF v0.2 verified draft (CH05 / CH06 object permanence and physical reasoning)

## What We Know

Parents frequently ask *when do babies get object permanence*? For half a century, the Piagetian orthodoxy held that infants do not acquire object permanence until approximately 8 to 9 months, when they first reach under a cloth to retrieve a hidden rattle, and that full mental representation remains incomplete until 18 to 24 months as evidenced by perseverative A-not-B reach errors (Piaget 1954). However, modern looking-time experiments demonstrated that young infants possess mental representations of hidden objects far earlier. Baillargeon (1987) demonstrated using rotating-drawbridge habituation displays that 3.5- to 4.5-month-old infants look significantly longer at impossible occlusion events (where a solid screen rotates 180 degrees through the space occupied by a hidden wooden box), proving that perceptual awareness of object solidity and permanence exists months before infants coordinate reaching hands. Diamond (1985) subsequently showed that the A-not-B search error does not stem from absent object permanence, but from the slow maturation of dorsolateral prefrontal cortex inhibitory control and motor working memory: infants look toward the correct hiding location B while their reaching hand perseverates to previously reinforced location A.

## How Confident We Are

**Grade**: Moderate (Laboratory Replication & Convergent Tasks) that violation-of-expectation looking indexes core physical expectations and object permanence in infants by 3.5 to 5 months, and that manual search failures reflect motor planning and inhibitory maturation rather than conceptual absence. **Low** that early looking-time sensitivity represents a finished, articulate causal theory or predicts subsequent childhood academic intelligence.

## Evidence and Causal Assessment

Experimental dissociation between looking and manual reaching. Baillargeon's violation-of-expectation (VOE) paradigm has been replicated across occlusion, containment, collision, and support configurations (Stahl & Feigenson 2015). Diamond's developmental progression across 7.5 to 12 months showed that increasing the delay between hiding and reaching produces systematic A-not-B perseveration until prefrontal inhibitory circuits mature. Search tasks and looking tasks dissociate because two-step means-end manual retrieval places heavy demands on immature motor executive coordination.

## Effect Size and Persistence

VOE looking-time differences between possible and impossible occlusion events are robust ($d = 0.50$ to $0.80$ in tightly controlled laboratory displays). Longitudinal tracking shows that early VOE sensitivity reflects normative cognitive architecture rather than an individual predictor of school-age IQ.

## Bias, Heterogeneity, and Confounding

GI attenuation: **n/a**. Methodological debates center on whether prolonged staring reflects rich conceptual representation versus low-level perceptual novelty or habituation dynamics (Aslin 2007; ManyBabies 2020).

## What Remains Uncertain

How laboratory physical expectations interface with real-world infant navigation and manual manipulation in complex, cluttered household environments.

## What This Does Not Mean

Does not mean young babies are miniature physicists who understand abstract gravity or mechanics. Does not mean failing to reach for a hidden toy at 6 months indicates cognitive delay. Does not mean commercial flashcards or baby-physics toys accelerate brain development.

## What Action Is Reasonably Justified

Parents do not need special cognitive drilling toys or apps. Natural interactive games (peekaboo, hiding a toy under a blanket, watching objects roll and fall) provide ample sensory-motor scaffolding matching the infant's natural developmental timetable.
