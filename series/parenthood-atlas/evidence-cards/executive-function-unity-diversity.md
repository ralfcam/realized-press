# Executive Function as Unity and Diversity

> Domain: executive function
> Last updated: 2026-09-16
> Status: Source-audited first pass (CH11-C01/C02). `self-regulation-delay-of-gratification.md` is reconciled, not rewritten.

## What We Know

Executive function (EF) names top-down processes that help maintain goal-directed behavior when a more automatic response is insufficient. Miyake et al. (2000) showed in adults that inhibition, updating, and shifting share variance *and* are separable. Garon, Bryson & Smith (2008) mapped an integrative, developmental framework for preschoolers: early forms of attention, inhibition, and working memory are visible before a stable adult factor structure. Toddler performance is a precursor, not miniature adult EF.

## How Confident We Are

**Grade**: Moderate-to-High for the broad construct of related control processes. **Moderate** for any specific factor structure before school age. **Low** that a single toddler task is a complete EF measure.

## Evidence and Causal Assessment

The evidence is psychometric, experimental, and developmental, not a causal law of parenting. Task performance includes language, speed, motor demands, motivation, and familiarity. Garon et al. (2008) is a review, not a new RCT. Delay-of-gratification prediction is a different, attenuated claim on the Watts card.

## Effect Size and Persistence

Age-related gains across early childhood are robust at the population level. Rank-order stability is lower in toddlerhood than later. A developmental increase is not a permanent individual difference.

## Bias, Heterogeneity, and Confounding

GI attenuation: **n/a** for the construct claim. Task impurity, low reliability, cultural familiarity, and language load are the main threats. SES–EF associations exist in other literatures and are not this card’s identification.

## What Remains Uncertain

Whether early EF is one common factor, several components, or a hierarchy; how much laboratory control transfers to household goals.

## What This Does Not Mean

EF is not intelligence, obedience, moral worth, or temperament. High compliance can occur without flexible goal maintenance. Poor task performance can reflect stress, language, or an invalid task. Early differences are not a verdict on children or parents.

## What Action Is Reasonably Justified

Use multiple observations across contexts. High-stakes ranking of toddlers by one EF task does not follow.
