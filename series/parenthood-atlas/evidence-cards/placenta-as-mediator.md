# The Placenta Mediates Fetal Dose

> Domain: pregnancy
> Last updated: 2026-09-15
> Status: Source-audited first pass (CH02-C02)

## What We Know

The placenta is a transient organ of exchange, metabolism, and endocrine signalling. Maternal blood contents are not a fetal dose. Placental 11β-HSD2 inactivates much maternal cortisol; the buffer is real and incomplete.

## How Confident We Are

**Grade**: Moderate-to-High for mediation as anatomy/physiology; **Low** that a placental biomarker forecasts childhood cognition or personality.

## Evidence and Causal Assessment

Burton & Fowden (2015) review transport, uterine-artery remodelling, endocrine modulation of maternal metabolism, and the placenta as a selective barrier. Seckl & Holmes (2007) review 11β-HSD2 as a fetoplacental glucocorticoid barrier: animal knockout/inhibition and human HSD11B2 mutations associate with reduced birthweight; this is mechanism plus selected human genetics, not a population stress-to-IQ path (see the stress card).

## Effect Size and Persistence

A placental enzyme level or weight is contemporaneous physiology. Lasting impairment does not follow from the existence of a pathway.

## Bias, Heterogeneity, and Confounding

GI attenuation: **n/a** (biological placental transport physiology, not an observational population phenotype association).

Placenta sampled at birth; reverse causation; maternal–fetal genotype; over-translation of programming animal models.

## What Remains Uncertain

How much typical variation in 11β-HSD2 changes fetal glucocorticoid exposure in ordinary pregnancies; transport of specific xenobiotics at consumer doses.

## What This Does Not Mean

Does not mean the mother “sends” every feeling or chemical to the child, or that placental markers are a personality test.

## What Action Is Reasonably Justified

Clinical attention to placental insufficiency and known teratogens is justified. Individual purification or guilt about ordinary maternal states is not licensed by this mechanism.
