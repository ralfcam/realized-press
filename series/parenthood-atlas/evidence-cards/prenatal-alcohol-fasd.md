# Prenatal Alcohol and Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorders

> Domain: pregnancy
> Last updated: 2026-09-15
> Status: Source-audited first pass (CH02-C09)

## What We Know

Prenatal alcohol exposure is causally implicated in a clinical continuum (FASD) of growth restriction, characteristic facial features, and neurobehavioral impairment. Severity is dose- and timing-dependent. A detected exposure is not a diagnosis; a neurobehavioral problem is not proof of alcohol causation.

## How Confident We Are

**Grade**: High that heavy/binge prenatal alcohol can produce FASD as a teratologic continuum; **Low** that any detected exposure equals FASD or a fixed IQ injury.

## Evidence and Causal Assessment

Hoyme et al. (2016) update diagnostic criteria from evaluation of more than 10,000 children and NIAAA-linked epidemiology (CIFASD, CoFASP): documented prenatal alcohol exposure, facial features, growth, CNS, and neurobehavior. This is clinical teratology and consensus diagnosis, not a randomized exposure trial. Human causal inference rests on dose, pattern, dysmorphology, and exclusion of competing causes.

## Effect Size and Persistence

FASD persistence is often substantial. Unmeasured low-level exposure does not inherit that effect size.

## Bias, Heterogeneity, and Confounding

GI attenuation: **n/a** (teratological toxicology, not an observational population phenotype association).

Exposure under-report; polysubstance use; SES and nutrition; stigma; diagnostic substitution.

## What Remains Uncertain

Thresholds for neurobehavioral-only outcomes; how postnatal environments modify expression.

## What This Does Not Mean

Does not mean every drink produces FASD, or that every developmental difficulty is alcohol-caused, or that affected children have a fixed fate.

## What Action Is Reasonably Justified

Avoiding alcohol in pregnancy and supporting affected children are justified. Shame as a developmental theory is not.
