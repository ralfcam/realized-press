# Supine Safe Sleep, Room-Sharing Duration, and Sleep Environment

> Domain: sleep
> Last updated: 2026-10-29
> Status: Preliminary (epidemiological and cohort evidence locked)

## What We Know

High-certainty epidemiological evidence shows that placing an infant wholly on the back (supine) on a firm, flat, non-inclined sleep surface free of soft bedding, pillows, and bumpers—and avoiding surface bed-sharing when a parent is exhausted, smoking, or impaired—substantially reduces the risk of Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) and accidental suffocation, with peak vulnerability concentrated between 1 and 4 months of age. By contrast, while room-sharing without bed-sharing in the first 6 months is protective, prospective cohort evidence (the INSIGHT study) suggests that continued room-sharing at 4 and 9 months is associated with less consolidated night sleep, more frequent night feedings, and higher odds of transitioning into reactive bed-sharing overnight.

## How Confident We Are

**Grade**: High for supine sleep position and a firm, clear sleep surface reducing sleep-related infant mortality (`moon-2022-sids`), while the incremental benefit of room-sharing past 6 months carries greater uncertainty (`paul-2017-insight`).

## Evidence and Causal Assessment

- **AAP 2022 Evidence Synthesis (`moon-2022-sids`):** Synthesizes case-control and population surveillance studies showing that prone (stomach) and side sleeping increase SIDS odds roughly 2- to 13-fold, particularly when infants accustomed to supine sleep are placed prone unaccustomed ("unaccustomed prone"). Once an infant can independently roll from back to stomach and stomach to back (typically 4–6 months), the infant does not need to be repositioned if the crib is completely clear of soft objects and swaddling has been discontinued.
- **INSIGHT Prospective Cohort (`paul-2017-insight`, N=230 mother-infant dyads):** Infants who slept in their own room by 4 months or between 4 and 9 months obtained longer longest-sleep stretches (+45 to +46 minutes at 9 months) and more total night sleep at 4, 9, and 30 months than infants who remained in the parents' room at 9 months, and were four times less likely to be brought into the adult bed overnight.

## Effect Size and Persistence

Population-wide "Back to Sleep" campaigns reduced SIDS rates by more than 50% across high-income nations in the 1990s (`moon-2022-sids`). In the INSIGHT cohort (`paul-2017-insight`), independent sleepers at 9 months slept an average of 10 hours and 37 minutes at night versus 9 hours and 47 minutes for room-sharers, with differences in bedtime routines and night-sleep duration still detectable at 30 months.

## Bias, Heterogeneity, and Confounding

GI attenuation: **partially confounded (20–50%)** for room-sharing duration and sleep consolidation (`paul-2017-insight`), as parents of more wakeful infants may delay moving them to a separate room (reverse causation), though the INSIGHT study adjusted for baseline infant fussiness and feeding mode. By contrast, the mechanical and physiological hazard of prone sleep on soft bedding in early infancy is **robust (<20%)**.

## What Remains Uncertain

The exact causal inflection point between 4 and 12 months where the baseline statistical risk of SIDS in a low-risk, non-smoking, full-term infant becomes small enough that sleep-fragmentation trade-offs dominate, and optimal acoustic thresholds for infant sound machines (clinical occupational standards recommend keeping continuous white noise at or below 50 dBA measured at the crib and placed at least 2 meters / 7 feet away).

## What This Does Not Mean

Does not mean parents must wake up all night to flip a 6-month-old who has rolled onto their stomach on their own in an empty crib, provided arms-in swaddling has been stopped. It also does not mean moving an infant to a nursery at 5 or 6 months is reckless when all core safe-sleep rules (back to sleep, firm flat mattress, empty crib, smoke-free home) are followed.

## What Action Is Reasonably Justified

Always place infants on their backs on a firm, flat, empty crib or bassinet for every sleep, stop swaddling as soon as the infant shows signs of attempting to roll (typically 8–12 weeks), share a room (on a separate surface) for at least the first 4 to 6 months when SIDS risk is highest, and treat the transition to a separate nursery after 6 months—and the use of low-volume (<50 dBA) white noise placed across the room—as pragmatic household choices rather than moral tests.
