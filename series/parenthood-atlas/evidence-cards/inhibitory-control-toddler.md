# Toddler Inhibitory Control Is Context-Bound

> Domain: executive function
> Last updated: 2026-09-16
> Status: Source-audited first pass (CH11-C03). Delay-of-gratification card not rewritten.

## What We Know

Early forms of stopping or delaying a prepotent response are visible in infancy and become more reliable through toddlerhood and preschool. Garon, Bryson & Smith (2008) treat inhibition as a developing component, not a switch from impulse to control. Carlson (2005) catalogued age-sensitive tasks. Carlson, Davis & Leach (2005) showed that three-year-olds wait or choose better when the reward is represented symbolically (“Less Is More”) than when it is sitting there as a concrete temptation—evidence that inhibition is partly a representation problem, not a character trait.

## How Confident We Are

**Grade**: Moderate-to-High for age-related improvement in laboratory suppression tasks. **Moderate** for any single task as “the” inhibition score. **Low** that marshmallow-style waiting forecasts life outcomes (see `self-regulation-delay-of-gratification.md`).

## Evidence and Causal Assessment

Age trends come from batteries and longitudinal task studies. Experimental cue, language, and symbolic-distancing manipulations show that performance moves with context. That is stronger than a trait interpretation. It is not an RCT of parenting. The Watts 2018 replication remains the identification for the original strong-predictor claim; this card does not reopen it.

## Effect Size and Persistence

Population age trends are large; individual scores fluctuate with fatigue, emotion, and familiarity. Early scores have limited precision as forecasts.

## Bias, Heterogeneity, and Confounding

GI attenuation: **n/a**. Language comprehension, reward value, and adult scaffolding are confounds *and* supports. Play-as-EF-training is a different, weaker claim (`pretend-play-educational-claims.md`).

## What Remains Uncertain

How much laboratory suppression transfers to household conflict; which supports help without becoming a compliance drill.

## What This Does Not Mean

Failure to inhibit is not bad parenting, weak character, or absence of learning. Toddlers are not expected to apply a rule across emotional and unfamiliar contexts. Waiting longer is not superior character.

## What Action Is Reasonably Justified

Make rules concrete and reduce unnecessary temptation; these are proportionate supports. Ranking children by inhibition tasks, or training marshmallow performance, does not follow.
