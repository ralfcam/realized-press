# Maternal Tobacco Smoke and Fetal Growth

> Domain: pregnancy
> Last updated: 2026-09-15
> Status: Source-audited first pass (CH02-C10)

## What We Know

Active maternal cigarette smoking is associated with reduced fetal growth and higher preterm risk, with a reported dose-response. This is a birth-size and gestational-duration claim. It is not a personality or IQ card.

## How Confident We Are

**Grade**: Moderate for a causal contribution to reduced birthweight in observational syntheses with dose-response; not genetically informed in the dossier. Long-run cognition is a different, weaker claim.

## Evidence and Causal Assessment

Kramer (1987) assessed 43 determinants across 895 studies: in developed-country settings, cigarette smoking was the most important identified modifiable determinant of low birthweight, with effects on both growth restriction and gestational duration. Later biomarker work (not catalogued here as a second ID) often shows tighter dose-response with cotinine than with self-report. This remains epidemiology plus dose-response, not an RCT of smoking assignment.

## Effect Size and Persistence

Birthweight deficits are typically on the order of 150–250 g for active smoking in older syntheses, larger at higher consumption. Persistence of size difference into later life is mixed and confounded; do not carry the birthweight effect into IQ.

## Bias, Heterogeneity, and Confounding

GI attenuation: **partially confounded (20–50%)** (maternal smoking during pregnancy shows partial attenuation in sibling and Mendelian randomization studies).

SES, co-exposures, partner smoking, nutrition, residual confounding. Genetically informed designs for this specific path were not in the Chapter 2 dossier.

## What Remains Uncertain

How much of the association is nicotine vs combustion products vs correlated behaviors; magnitude of partner/passive exposure after active smoking is accounted for.

## What This Does Not Mean

Does not mean a smaller baby has a lesser future, or that smoking is the only growth constraint.

## What Action Is Reasonably Justified

Population and clinical support for smoking cessation in pregnancy is justified on birthweight/preterm grounds. Treating birth size as destiny is not.
