# Parent-Implemented Language Interventions

> Domain: language
> Last updated: 2026-09-16
> Status: Source-audited first pass (CH07 parent-implemented)

## What We Know

Because early language is learned in caregiver–child interaction, some interventions train parents rather than only treating the child in clinic. Heidlage et al. (2020) meta-analyzed **25 RCTs** (1,734 children, mean age 3.7 years) with or at risk for language impairment, including low-SES risk. Average effects: modest gains in **expressive vocabulary** (shared book reading *g* = 0.37; play/routines *g* = 0.50); small effects on broader expressive language; receptive language often nonsignificant. Dialogic reading (Whitehurst et al. 1988) is a named shared-book method inside that family, not a separate law.

## How Confident We Are

**Grade**: Moderate for modest expressive-vocabulary benefit in children with or at risk for language impairment under trial conditions. **Low** that the same packages raise later IQ in typical development, that LENA coaching *causes* Gilkerson’s school-age prediction (`conversational-turns-language.md`), or that this is a verdict on screens (`screen-time-language-development.md`).

## Evidence and Causal Assessment

RCTs of parent-implemented protocols. Efficacy under trial is not community effectiveness, scalability, or a universal parenting recipe (`CONTRIBUTING.md` intervention rule). Child speech is part of measured interaction; parent outcome measurement is often thin.

## Effect Size and Persistence

Hedges *g* in the small-to-moderate range for expressive vocabulary in the pooled trials. Persistence beyond post-test is unevenly reported. Dialogic reading’s original samples were small.

## Bias, Heterogeneity, and Confounding

GI attenuation: **n/a** (randomized assignment). Effects are smaller or uncertain for some autism-spectrum samples in the same meta-analysis. Families who enroll in RCTs are not all families.

## What Remains Uncertain

Active ingredients (book reading vs routines vs coaching dose); whether LENA-based feedback adds anything beyond interaction coaching; typical-development use.

## What This Does Not Mean

Does not mean typical homes must run a language curriculum. Does not mean a missed shared book writes a life. Does not re-open Hart & Risley as a causal metric. Does not treat screen time as the inverse of these trials.

## What Action Is Reasonably Justified

Targeted parent-implemented support for children with or at risk for language impairment is proportionate to the RCT package. Universal optimization of every toddler’s vocabulary is not.
