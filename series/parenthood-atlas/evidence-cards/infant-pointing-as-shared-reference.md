# Pointing Is Shared Reference, Not a Language Product

> Domain: language / social
> Last updated: 2026-09-16
> Status: Source-audited first pass (CH07-C05). Joint attention is a different card.

## What We Know

Pointing can coordinate two minds around one referent. Colonnesi, Stams, Koster & Noom (2010) found pointing associated with language concurrently (*r* = .52) and longitudinally (*r* = .35) across 25 studies (734 children), for declarative/general pointing, not imperative pointing. Commercial baby-sign packages for typically developing hearing infants are a different claim: Fitzpatrick et al. (2014) found inconclusive language advantages and no evidence of harm.

## How Confident We Are

**Grade**: Moderate that pointing functions as shared reference and predicts later language. **Low** that teaching arbitrary signs in typical hearing samples accelerates spoken language, or that a child who points less is thereby delayed.

## Evidence and Causal Assessment

Colonnesi et al. (2010), *Developmental Review*, is an observational meta-analysis. Shared attention, motor, and learning capacities can drive both gesture and speech. Longitudinal *r* = .35 is prediction, not an RCT of “more pointing.” Fitzpatrick, Thibert, Grandpierre & Johnston (2014) reviewed gestural-communication programs in typically developing hearing infants under 36 months: language benefits inconclusive; one randomized study found no advantage on standardized measures. Joint attention (Mundy & Newell) is not re-extracted here.

## Effect Size and Persistence

Concurrent association is large by social-development standards; longitudinal association is moderate. Neither is a treatment effect for a marketed curriculum.

## Bias, Heterogeneity, and Confounding

GI attenuation: **n/a** (developmental communicative milestone, not an observational population phenotype association).

Age, pointing motive, and language modality moderate the Colonnesi estimates. SES and country were examined as moderators; they do not license a parental purity test. Baby-sign trials are small and mixed. Genetically informed attenuation: **n/a**.

## What Remains Uncertain

Whether increasing pointing in ordinary homes causes vocabulary growth; how far pointing tasks generalize across caregiving cultures.

## What This Does Not Mean

Does not mean a quiet pointer is delayed. Does not mean baby-sign products are required, harmful, or equivalent to naturally emerging gesture. Does not mean Hart & Risley.

## What Action Is Reasonably Justified

Treat pointing and showing as ordinary shared reference. Purchasing a sign package to accelerate typical speech is not justified here.
