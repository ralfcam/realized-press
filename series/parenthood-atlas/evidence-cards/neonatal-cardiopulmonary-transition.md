# Neonatal Cardiopulmonary Transition

> Domain: birth
> Last updated: 2026-09-16
> Status: Stable for the *narrow* typical-term sequence claim (`stable-candidates--replication-note.md`). Timing of each shunt remains Moderate.

## What We Know

After placental separation, gas exchange must move from placenta to lungs. Lung aeration and oxygenation lower pulmonary vascular resistance; loss of the low-resistance placental circuit raises systemic vascular resistance. Those pressure changes drive functional closure of the foramen ovale, ductus arteriosus, and ductus venosus. Metabolic and endocrine support (cortisol, catecholamines, glucose) is part of the same transition, not a separate personality system.

## How Confident We Are

**Grade**: High for the direction of the respiratory–circulatory sequence in typical term transition; **Moderate** for exact timing of each shunt and for the relative contribution of individual mediators in humans. Identification is mechanistic and clinical, not randomized.

## Evidence and Causal Assessment

Morton & Brodsky (2016) review fetal circulation (ductus venosus, foramen ovale, ductus arteriosus), lung-fluid clearance, and the postnatal rise in systemic and fall in pulmonary resistance. Hillman, Kallapur & Jobe (2012) independently describe the same sequence: lung aeration as the initiator, falling pulmonary and rising systemic resistance, and functional rather than instantaneous anatomical shunt closure. Functional flap closure of the foramen ovale follows the left-atrial pressure rise; ductal constriction is oxygen-sensitive. Human experiments cannot ethically withhold the first breath; illness, prematurity, and asphyxia are natural contrasts and are confounded by treatment.

## Effect Size and Persistence

The immediate outcome is oxygenation and a viable circulation. Transient respiratory or shunt findings do not inherit a long-run cognitive or temperament effect size. Persistence of later risk must be measured separately and is strongly modified by gestational age, illness, and subsequent care.

## Bias, Heterogeneity, and Confounding

GI attenuation: **n/a**. Preterm birth and cesarean without labor alter cortisol and catecholamine patterns; asphyxia changes the physiology of transition. Diagrams that treat “closure” as a single instant oversimplify functional versus anatomical change.

## What Remains Uncertain

How much ordinary variation in first-hour adaptation forecasts later health once gestational age and illness are accounted for.

## What This Does Not Mean

A difficult first breath or a temporarily open shunt is not a developmental sentence. A smooth transition is not evidence of superior future cognition or character. Caregivers do not replace the placenta as an organ.

## What Action Is Reasonably Justified

Trained assessment of breathing, warmth, and resuscitation when indicated is justified. Using neonatal transition as a personality origin story is not.
