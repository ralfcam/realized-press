# Educational Claims About Pretend Play Exceed the Evidence

> Domain: play
> Last updated: 2026-09-16
> Status: Source-audited first pass (CH12-C10). Exploration, embodiment, and peer cards are neighbors; preschool RCT card is not rewritten.

## What We Know

Pretend play has been claimed to be crucial for healthy development. Lillard et al. (2013) reviewed experimental and correlational evidence for language, narrative, emotion regulation, theory of mind, social skills, and executive function. They concluded that the evidence does not support strong causal claims about unique importance, and that equifinality—multiple routes to similar outcomes—is a serious alternative. Pretend play can recruit dual representation, inhibition of literal reading, memory, flexibility, and language. Those demands do not prove unique causation.

## How Confident We Are

**Grade**: High that sweeping educational claims exceed the evidence. **Moderate** that pretense has real cognitive demands. **Low** that ordinary pretend play independently causes durable EF, ToM, creativity, or IQ gains.

## Evidence and Causal Assessment

Lillard et al. (2013) is a systematic narrative review of a large, uneven literature, including null and nonreplicated experimental findings and masked-assessor cautions. Association between pretense and EF is compatible with bidirectionality and selection. Guided-play curricula and landmark preschool RCTs (`preschool-early-education-rct.md`) are different packages; they are not “pretend play” as such. Chapter 11 play-as-EF remains Low-to-Moderate on that dossier; this card holds the causal claim down.

## Effect Size and Persistence

No stable, generalizable effect estimate currently justifies claiming durable, cross-domain gains from pretend play. Short-term task bumps under particular story conditions should not be generalized.

## Bias, Heterogeneity, and Confounding

GI attenuation: **n/a**. Children with stronger language, EF, and adult support both pretend more complexly and score higher on developmental tasks. Definitions of “play” vary. Publication bias and small samples are live.

## What Remains Uncertain

Whether specified, high-quality pretend-play *interventions* yield domain-specific improvements that transfer and persist.

## What This Does Not Mean

Pretend play is not irrelevant. The rejected claim is unique necessity and general cognitive enhancement. Child-led play is not proven EF training. More scheduled “educational play” is not automatically better.

## What Action Is Reasonably Justified

Protect time and materials for imaginative activity without turning play into a performance target. Using play as a universal intelligence or ToM prescription does not follow.
