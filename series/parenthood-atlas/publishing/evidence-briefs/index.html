Behavioral Inhibition as a Probabilistic Anxiety Marker
Early behavioral inhibition is associated with later social-anxiety risk at the group level; it is neither necessary nor sufficient, and stranger wariness is not a diagnosis.		 MODERATE-GROUP-RISK; LOW-INDIVIDUAL-FORECAST emotion, temperament HTML PDF
Breastfeeding and Cognitive Development
PROBIT RCT and sibling-comparison evidence indicate a small, verbal-concentrated, fading causal effect of breastfeeding on IQ.		 MODERATE feeding, cognition HTML PDF
Childhood Self-Control Is a Population Gradient, Not a Sentence
In the Dunedin cohort, childhood self-control predicts later health, wealth, and public-safety outcomes at the group level; improvement changes standing, and this is not a marshmallow-test replication.		 MODERATE-GRADIENT; LOW-INDIVIDUAL-FORECAST regulation, life-course HTML PDF
Committed Compliance Is Not the Same as Obedience
Kochanska’s observational program distinguishes committed (willing) compliance from situational compliance; the former is more closely linked to later internalization, and neither is a moral verdict or a parenting score.		 MODERATE-DISTINCTION; LOW-TECHNIQUE behavior, regulation HTML PDF
Conversational Turns Predict Later Language Better Than Raw Word Counts
In LENA recordings, adult–child conversational turns at 18–24 months predict school-age language and IQ after SES control more robustly than adult word counts; 18-month expressive vocabulary norms (CDI median ~50 words, range 10-170+) show vast typical variance.		 MODERATE-PREDICTION; LOW-CAUSATION language HTML PDF
Deferred Imitation After a Delay
Fourteen-month-olds can reproduce novel multi-step actions on objects after delays of up to one week without prior motor practice; this establishes preverbal recall memory, not neonatal imitation or an accelerated copying curriculum.		 HIGH-FOURTEEN-MONTH-ONE-WEEK; LOW-NEONATE-AND-TRANSFER learning, memory HTML PDF
Mode of Delivery and Neonatal Physiological Transition
Cesarean delivery alters immediate neonatal lung fluid clearance and neuroendocrine stress surges; observational associations with long-term neurodevelopment, autism, and cognitive traits attenuate to null under sibling-comparison designs.		 HIGH-IMMEDIATE-PHYSIOLOGY; LOW-LONG-TERM-CAUSATION birth, neonatal HTML PDF
Two Languages Do Not Confuse Young Children
Simultaneous dual-language exposure from birth does not confuse infants, impair communicative milestones, or delay core speech discrimination; vocabulary is distributed across languages, and bilingualism does not automatically confer an executive function bonus.		 HIGH-NO-CONFUSION; MODERATE-INDIVIDUAL-TRAJECTORIES; LOW-AUTOMATIC-BONUS language, learning HTML PDF
Early Allergen Introduction and Baby-Led Weaning
Randomized trials show that introducing peanut protein between 4 and 11 months reduces peanut allergy by ~80% in high-risk infants (LEAP), while modified baby-led weaning and traditional spoon-feeding produce similar growth and choking rates (BLISS).		 HIGH feeding, health HTML PDF
Early Childcare Quality and Development
NICHD SECCYD cohort shows modest, replicated links between childcare quality and cognitive/language outcomes, with parenting a stronger predictor; cortisol elevations in daycare are small (d ~ 0.15-0.30) and buffered by caregiver sensitivity.		 MODERATE parenting, ecology, early-learning HTML PDF
Motor Development Is Embodied Perception–Action Coupling
Opportunities for infant action depend on the current body, the environment, and social practice; new motor skills change what can be learned, but earlier sitting or walking is not a general cognitive advantage or a parenting score.		 MODERATE-FRAMEWORK; LOW-MILESTONE-COGNITION learning HTML PDF
Executive Function as Unity and Diversity
Executive function is a family of related but not identical control processes (inhibition, working memory, shifting); early performance is context-bound and is not intelligence, obedience, or character.		 MODERATE-TO-HIGH-CONSTRUCT; MODERATE-TODDLER-STRUCTURE; LOW-SINGLE-TASK executive-function, cognition HTML PDF
Exploration as Sampling: Why Babies Drop Things from High Chairs and Test Physical Rules
Infants drop spoons, toys, and food from high chairs not out of defiance, but as active empirical sampling; surprise from violated physical expectations reorganizes directed exploration and hypothesis testing.		 MODERATE-LAB; LOW-TRANSFER learning, motor-cognition HTML PDF
Fathers, Kin, and Siblings Are Heterogeneous Network Members
Positive father involvement is associated with early learning at small-to-moderate average size; involvement is not one exposure, and fathers, kin, and siblings are not interchangeable doses of care.		 MODERATE-ASSOCIATION; LOW-UNIQUE-CAUSE social, family HTML PDF
Fetal Autonomic Behavior Indexes Present Maturation
Fetal heart-rate variability and state organization become more patterned in the second half of gestation; these measures support claims about present capacity, not reliable individual forecasts of temperament.		 MODERATE-PRESENT; LOW-FORECAST pregnancy HTML PDF
Fetal Growth Restriction Is a Clinical Outcome, Not a Destiny
INTERGROWTH-21st provides international fetal-size standards; severe restriction is clinically meaningful, but birth size is not a proxy for intelligence, worth, or future personality.		 MODERATE-STANDARDS; HIGH-PERCENTILE-NOT-DESTINY pregnancy HTML PDF
Guided Play Efficacy and Pedagogical Scaffolding
Adult-guided play shows targeted advantages over direct instruction for early mathematics and shape concepts, and over free play for spatial vocabulary; evidence does not show broad benefits for literacy, general executive function, or mandatory home instruction.		 MODERATE-MATH-SPATIAL; LOW-LITERACY-AND-HOME-CURRICULUM play, early-learning HTML PDF
Household Food Insecurity Is Associated with Infant and Toddler Health, Not Parental Failure
In a low-income US clinical sample, household food insecurity is associated with poorer infant and toddler health; the association is not a parenting residual or a cognitive-intervention law.		 MODERATE-ASSOCIATION; LOW-COGNITION-LAW ecology, health HTML PDF
Income Support Is Not a Guaranteed Cognitive Intervention
Poverty is associated with developmental disadvantage through multiple pathways; Baby’s First Years shows a defined monthly cash transfer can change infant brain activity without producing preregistered age-four cognitive gains.		 MODERATE-GRADIENT; LOW-TO-MODERATE-CASH-COGNITION ecology, policy HTML PDF
Infant Attention Is a Family of Processes, Not a Trait
Attention in infancy comprises dissociable functions (alertness, orienting, feature attention, endogenous control) that change on different schedules; inattention in a given hour is not a character verdict.		 MODERATE-FUNCTIONS; LOW-TRAIT cognition HTML PDF
Babbling Is Exploration, Not an IQ Test
Canonical babbling is motor, auditory, and social practice; late onset can flag later production risk in screened samples and is not a verdict on typical intelligence or parenting.		 HIGH-CANONICAL-STAGE; LOW-IQ-VERDICT language HTML PDF
Infant–Caregiver Co-Regulation Is Bidirectional
Co-regulation is a bidirectional, context-sensitive process in which infant and caregiver organize arousal together; synchrony is not uniformly beneficial, and nurturing care is an ecology, not a maternal purity test.		 MODERATE-EXISTENCE; LOW-TO-MODERATE-MECHANISM regulation, attachment HTML PDF
Infant Contingency Learning
Infants can learn that a self-generated action predicts a sensory consequence in the mobile-conjugate-reinforcement paradigm; this is action–outcome learning under controlled conditions, not adult-like causal theory or a stimulation mandate.		 HIGH-CONTROLLED; LOW-TRANSFER learning, play HTML PDF
Infant Crying Is a Graded Signal, Not a Code
Crying recruits care and conveys degree of distress; it is not a reliable one-to-one code for hunger, pain, or parental error, and ordinary inability to ‘decode’ the sound is not failure.		 MODERATE-SIGNAL; LOW-ACOUSTIC-DECODING birth, regulation HTML PDF
Infants Can Form Attachments to More Than One Caregiver
Infants can organize relationship-specific attachment patterns with more than one caregiver; network size is not a dose, and a second caregiver is not automatically better.		 MODERATE-OCCURRENCE; LOW-NETWORK-DOSE attachment, social HTML PDF
Infant Perceptual Narrowing for Speech Contrasts
During the first year, discrimination of native-language phonetic contrasts is maintained or strengthened while sensitivity to some nonnative contrasts declines; this is attunement, not a permanent closing of language learning.		 HIGH-LAB; MODERATE-TIMETABLE; LOW-SEALED-DEADLINE language HTML PDF
Early Physical Reasoning and Object Permanence: Looking vs. Manual Search
Young infants demonstrate object permanence and physical reasoning in looking-time tasks months before manual cloth retrieval; search failure reflects frontostriatal inhibitory and motor planning immaturity rather than absent representation.		 MODERATE-VOE; LOW-COMPLETE-THEORY learning, cognition HTML PDF
Pointing Is Shared Reference, Not a Language Product
Declarative pointing is concurrently and longitudinally associated with language; commercial baby-sign packages are a different, inconclusive claim.		 MODERATE-REFERENCE; LOW-PRODUCT language, social HTML PDF
Infant Sleep Training: Graduated Extinction & The Ferber Method
RCT and cohort evidence finds no measured attachment or behavioral harm from behavioral sleep training, graduated extinction (the Ferber method), and cry-it-out through age 6.		 LOW-TO-MODERATE regulation, health, parenting HTML PDF
Infant Social Referencing Under Uncertainty
Late in the first year, infants can use a familiar adult’s emotional signal to regulate approach or withdrawal in an ambiguous situation; referencing is not an attachment classification.		 HIGH-LAB; LOW-ATTACHMENT-VERDICT social, regulation HTML PDF
Infant Statistical Speech Segmentation
Eight-month-olds can use transitional probabilities in a brief artificial-speech stream to distinguish word-like units from part-words; this is laboratory segmentation, not proof that statistics alone explain natural language.		 HIGH-LAB; LOW-TRANSFER language, learning HTML PDF
Still-Face Disruption of Expected Social Contingency
When a familiar adult suddenly becomes unresponsive in face-to-face interaction, infants typically protest, look away, and try to re-engage; this is a laboratory signature of expected contingency, not a diagnosis of attachment or co-regulation failure.		 HIGH-LAB; LOW-ATTACHMENT-VERDICT emotion, regulation HTML PDF
Toddler Inhibitory Control Is Context-Bound
Response suppression becomes more reliable across toddlerhood and preschool under particular cues and supports; failure to inhibit is not character, and delay-of-gratification destiny claims belong on the Watts card.		 MODERATE-TO-HIGH-AGE-TREND; MODERATE-TASK; LOW-MARSHMALLOW-DESTINY executive-function, regulation HTML PDF
Instrumental Helping Is Not Mature Altruism
In the second year, children sometimes help an adult complete a simple goal; this laboratory emergence is not mature altruism, a character forecast, or a parenting score.		 MODERATE-EMERGENCE; LOW-CHARACTER-FORECAST social, behavior HTML PDF
Joint Attention Is Shared Reference, Not a Language Guarantee
Joint attention is the coordinated processing of one’s own attention, another’s attention, and a shared referent; associations with later communication are real and not a proof that drilling gaze-following produces language.		 MODERATE-CONSTRUCT; LOW-ISOLATED-TRAINING cognition, language HTML PDF
Looking Time Is a Measurement, Not a Window into Thought
Infant looking-time methods reliably index perceptual discrimination, novelty, and attentional salience under controlled conditions; looking duration is not a transparent readout of rich conceptual knowledge, belief, or innate theories.		 HIGH-METHOD; LOW-RICH-INTERPRETATION cognition HTML PDF
Neighborhood Moves Have Age-Dependent Later Effects
Moving to a lower-poverty neighborhood before adolescence increased later college attendance and adult earnings in Moving to Opportunity; this is an adult-economic finding, not a toddler-development law, and moving is not universally beneficial.		 MODERATE-LATER-EARNINGS; LOW-TO-MODERATE-EARLY-DEV; LOW-EVERY-CHARACTERISTIC ecology, policy HTML PDF
Neonatal Cardiopulmonary Transition
Birth reorganizes gas exchange and circulation after placental separation; shunt closure is functional, not instant destiny, and a difficult transition is not a personality forecast.		 HIGH-SEQUENCE; MODERATE-TIMING birth, regulation HTML PDF
Neonatal Tongue-Tie (Ankyloglossia) and Frenotomy
Cochrane review of randomized trials finds that surgical tongue-tie release (frenotomy) modestly reduces short-term maternal nipple pain in classic anterior ankyloglossia, but shows no consistent effect on long-term breastfeeding duration or infant weight gain.		 MODERATE feeding, health HTML PDF
Object Permanence Is Not a Single Staircase
Looking-time studies report sensitivity to occlusion events in young infants; usable search appears later and depends on action demands. Looking and reaching measure different layers, not one completed object concept.		 MODERATE-LOOKING-SENSITIVITY; LOW-COMPLETE-CONCEPT cognition HTML PDF
Resilience Often Arises from Ordinary Adaptive Systems
Resilience in development commonly depends on ordinary systems—relationships, regulation, competence, and community resources—not rare invulnerability; adversity is not harmless.		 HIGH-EXISTENCE; MODERATE-MECHANISM; LOW-COSTLESS resilience, systems HTML PDF
Risky Outdoor Play Is Not a 0–3 Developmental Law
A systematic review finds associations between risky outdoor play and some health outcomes in children, mostly older than this atlas’s window; it is not a toddler efficacy package or a license for unsupervised hazard.		 MODERATE-SCHOOL-AGE-ASSOCIATION; LOW-TO-MODERATE-0-3; LOW-TODDLER-PACKAGE play, health HTML PDF
Parent-Implemented Language Interventions
Across 25 RCTs, parent-implemented language interventions produce modest gains in expressive vocabulary for young children with or at risk for language impairment; this is not a typical-development vocabulary mandate and is not a screen-time claim.		 MODERATE-EXPRESSIVE-VOCAB; LOW-UNIVERSAL-TYPICAL language HTML PDF
Parental Leave Is a Heterogeneous Package, Not a Unitary Exposure
A Norwegian maternity-leave reform is associated with better long-run child outcomes; leave protects maternal physiological recovery, where extreme sleep debt predicts postpartum rage, irritability, and mood disturbance requiring clinical recognition and treatment.		 MODERATE-REFORM; LOW-TO-MODERATE-0-3; LOW-UNITARY-JOB ecology, policy HTML PDF
Paternal Age and De Novo Mutation Rate
De novo single-nucleotide mutations in offspring increase linearly with paternal age at conception; this establishes a biological germline mutation rate, not an individual psychiatric or cognitive forecast for a child.		 HIGH-MUTATION-RATE; LOW-FORECAST preconception, pregnancy, genetics HTML PDF
Physical Punishment Is Not a Developmental Necessity
Prospective syntheses associate spanking with worse child behavior and find no reliable positive developmental benefit; this is not a claim that every instance produces a damaged child.		 MODERATE-TO-HIGH-NOT-NECESSARY; MODERATE-EFFECT-SIZE behavior, family HTML PDF
The Placenta Mediates Fetal Dose
Maternal exposure is not equivalent to fetal dose; the placenta transports, metabolizes, restricts, or facilitates substances, including via an incomplete glucocorticoid buffer.		 MODERATE-TO-HIGH-MECHANISM; LOW-CHILD-FORECAST pregnancy HTML PDF
Positional Plagiocephaly and Cranial Orthotic Helmet Therapy
Randomized controlled trial evidence demonstrates that custom cranial orthotic helmets provide no added skull-rounding benefit over natural course for moderate-to-severe infant plagiocephaly by age 2.		 HIGH-FOR-LACK-OF-ADDED-BENEFIT-OVER-NATURAL-COURSE; LOW-FOR-SEVERE-SYNDROMIC-CRANIOSYNOSTOSIS physical-development, health, motor-development HTML PDF
Preconception Nutrition Beyond Folate Is Not a Second NTD Trial
Reviews of preconception nutritional risks find a mixed, nutrient-specific evidence base; folate’s High/stable NTD identification does not generalize to other preconception nutrients or to later IQ.		 LOW-TO-MODERATE-BEYOND-FOLATE; LOW-IQ-CHECKLIST preconception, nutrition HTML PDF
Prenatal Alcohol and Fetal Alcohol Spectrum Disorders
Prenatal alcohol is causally implicated in FASD spanning growth, facial, and neurobehavioral outcomes; detected exposure is not equivalent to FASD, and not all neurobehavioral problems are alcohol-caused.		 HIGH-FASD; LOW-ANY-SIP pregnancy HTML PDF
Prenatal Auditory Discrimination and Newborn Preference
Late-pregnancy speech exposure can shape newborn auditory preference and neonatal ERP memory traces; this is sensory discrimination and neural encoding, not evidence of complex prenatal cognition or lasting cognitive advantage.		 MODERATE-PREFERENCE; LOW-LASTING-ADVANTAGE pregnancy, language, perception HTML PDF
Prenatal Developmental Timing Is Process-Specific
The same prenatal exposure can have different effects depending on dose, route, and the developmental process underway; pregnancy is not a single window.		 MODERATE-PRINCIPLE; HIGH-NO-SINGLE-WINDOW pregnancy HTML PDF
Prenatal Environmental Mixtures and Placental Markers
Mixture studies can associate phthalates and related chemicals with placental biomarkers; this does not establish that typical consumer-level detection causes permanent child injury.		 LOW-ASSOCIATION; VERY-LOW-PERMANENT-INJURY pregnancy HTML PDF
Periconceptional Folic Acid and Neural-Tube Defects
Randomized and systematic-review evidence shows periconceptional folic acid substantially reduces neural-tube-defect risk; this does not generalize to all anomalies or to intelligence.		 HIGH-NTD; LOW-OTHER-ANOMALIES-COGNITION pregnancy, nutrition HTML PDF
Prenatal Iodine, Maternal T4, and Neurodevelopment
Severe early iodine deficiency can disrupt neurodevelopment via maternal T4; late correction does not rewind early corticogenesis. This is not a brief for supplement maximalism in replete populations.		 HIGH-SEVERE; LOW-MILD-REPLETE pregnancy HTML PDF
Prenatal Maternal Stress and Anxiety
Observational prenatal stress/anxiety associations with child outcomes largely attenuate under genetically informed designs.		 LOW-TO-MODERATE-TYPICAL-RANGE; MODERATE-LARGELY-CONFOUNDED; LOW-RESIDUAL-PRENATAL pregnancy, development HTML PDF
Prenatal Neural Construction Is Unfinished at Birth
Human cortical development proceeds through overlapping proliferation, migration, and transient zones; the brain remains immature at birth and continues under postnatal experience.		 HIGH-STRUCTURE; LOW-COGNITION-FORECAST pregnancy HTML PDF
Maternal Tobacco Smoke and Fetal Growth
Active maternal tobacco smoking is associated with lower birthweight and higher preterm risk, with dose-response evidence; this is a birth-size/preterm claim, not a personality or IQ card.		 MODERATE pregnancy HTML PDF
Preschool & Early Interventions: Perry, Abecedarian, and Fade-Out at Scale
Two small, high-fidelity 1960s-70s RCTs (Perry, Abecedarian) show large, persistent adult gains; modern at-scale preschool interventions often show cognitive fade-out.		 MODERATE-HISTORICAL; LOW-GENERALIZATION early-learning, cognition, policy HTML PDF
Educational Claims About Pretend Play Exceed the Evidence
Pretend play is a real developmental activity with plausible cognitive demands; evidence does not establish that it uniquely causes intelligence, executive function, theory of mind, or creativity.		 HIGH-OVERCLAIM; MODERATE-DEMANDS; LOW-UNIQUE-CAUSE play, executive-function HTML PDF
Preterm Birth Raises Group-Level Cognitive Risk, Not a Sentence
A 2024 meta-analysis finds preterm birth associated with lower global cognitive scores (SMD about −0.57); the difference is a population risk, concentrated among the most immature and those with pulmonary morbidity, not an individual forecast.		 MODERATE-GROUP; LOW-INDIVIDUAL birth HTML PDF
Early Rank-Order Continuity Is Modest, Not Destiny
Personality and temperament show modest rank-order consistency in early childhood that increases with age; a three-year-old’s relative standing is not adult personality.		 MODERATE-TO-HIGH-PATTERN; LOW-INDIVIDUAL-FORECAST development, temperament HTML PDF
Responsive Caregiving and Attachment Interventions
Randomized sensitivity-training interventions reliably raise caregiver sensitivity and, to a lesser degree, infant attachment security.		 MODERATE-TO-HIGH-SENSITIVITY; MODERATE-ATTACHMENT attachment, parenting, social HTML PDF
Feeding as Reciprocal Regulation, Not a Transfer of Calories
Responsive feeding is a cue-based, bidirectional process inside ordinary caregiving; it is not a weight or IQ program, and it is not the suck–swallow or breastfeeding-cognition claim.		 MODERATE-FRAMEWORK; LOW-GROWTH-PROGRAM regulation, feeding HTML PDF
Family Routines, Predictability, and Adult Modeling
Predictable family routines and consistent adult behavioral modeling provide proximal scaffolding that supports toddler self-regulation and sleep; routines function as supportive environmental structure, not as a rigid causal machine that installs compliance or conscience.		 MODERATE-SCAFFOLDING; LOW-DIRECT-CAUSAL-CONSCIENCE parenting, self-regulation HTML PDF
Supine Safe Sleep, Room-Sharing Duration, and Sleep Environment
Placing infants on their backs on a firm, clear surface sharply reduces SIDS mortality, whereas extending room-sharing past 4 to 6 months shows lower-certainty benefits and is associated with shorter consolidated night sleep in the INSIGHT cohort.		 HIGH sleep, health HTML PDF
Screen Time and Language Development
Screen exposure is a decomposable, mostly observational predictor of language: quantity is negatively associated, co-viewing and educational content buffer.		 LOW-TO-MODERATE language, ecology, parenting HTML PDF
Self-Regulation and Delay of Gratification (Marshmallow Test)
A large NICHD-cohort replication shows the original marshmallow-test predictive claim is mostly explained by SES and early cognitive ability.		 MODERATE-TO-HIGH-OVERSTATEMENT-OF-ORIGINAL self-regulation, executive-function, cognition HTML PDF
Severe Institutional Deprivation Is Not Ordinary Parenting
Randomized foster-care placement after early institutional care improves some cognitive and relational outcomes; the contrast is severe deprivation, not typical variation in sensitivity or daycare.		 HIGH-DANGER; MODERATE-TO-HIGH-PLACEMENT; VERY-LOW-ORDINARY-CARE attachment, regulation, social HTML PDF
Early Skin-to-Skin Contact: Immediate Physiology, Not Character
In healthy newborns, early skin-to-skin contact is associated with higher breastfeeding rates and some short-term physiologic markers; GRADE is mixed, and the practice is not a lifelong character intervention.		 MODERATE-BREASTFEEDING; LOW-INFANT-PHYSIOLOGY birth, feeding, regulation HTML PDF
Oral Feeding Requires Suck–Swallow–Breathe Coordination
Safe oral feeding is a maturational coordination of sucking, swallowing, and respiration; the elements mature at different rates, especially in preterm infants, and feeding method is not parental worth.		 HIGH-COORDINATION; MODERATE-PRACTICE-EFFECTS birth, feeding HTML PDF
Symbolic and Pretend Play Emerge Across the Second Year
Object substitution and simple pretense commonly become visible between about 12 and 24 months; this is a representational transition, not a required intelligence program or a diagnostic cutoff.		 MODERATE-TO-HIGH-EMERGENCE; MODERATE-MECHANISM; LOW-DIAGNOSTIC-CUTOFF play, cognition HTML PDF
Temperament as Reactivity and Control, Not Destiny
Early-emerging differences in reactivity, approach, and attentional control are measurable and moderately stable; they are not a parenting verdict or a fixed personality forecast.		 MODERATE-DIFFERENCES; LOW-DESTINY emotion, temperament HTML PDF
Discipline and Behavioral Boundaries: Time-Outs, Physical Punishment, and Escalation
Physical punishment is associated with increased child aggression, externalizing problems, and antisocial behavior without promoting compliance; calm, brief time-out protocols serve as effective de-escalation tools without damaging attachment.		 MODERATE-SIBLING-TRIANGULATION; LOW-TECHNIQUE-ALONE social-emotional, boundaries HTML PDF
Toddler Peer Interaction Is Not a Social-Skills Commodity
In the second and third years, another toddler is a less accommodating partner than a caregiver; peer contexts create coordination demands, but hours of peer exposure do not automatically produce later prosocial competence.		 MODERATE-CONTEXT; LOW-HOURS-CAUSE social, play HTML PDF
Toddler Physical Aggression Usually Peaks and Declines
Physical aggression commonly rises through the second and third years and declines for most children; a smaller group remains elevated, and presence of hitting is not a violence forecast.		 MODERATE-TO-HIGH-TRAJECTORY; MODERATE-INDIVIDUAL; LOW-VIOLENCE-FORECAST behavior, social HTML PDF
Toilet Learning Readiness and Initiation Timing
Prospective cohort studies show that initiating intensive toilet training before 24 to 27 months lengthens total training duration by 8 to 12 months without advancing the final age of daytime dryness, which depends on physiological and behavioral readiness.		 MODERATE motor, self-regulation HTML PDF
Working Memory and Cognitive Shifting in Infancy and Toddlerhood
The capacity to maintain representations across delay and shift attentional sets develops systematically between 6 months and 3 years, linked to prefrontal maturation; task performance reflects emergent developmental capacity, not a fixed quotient of future intelligence.		 MODERATE-TO-HIGH-TRAJECTORY; LOW-INDIVIDUAL-FORECAST cognition, executive-function HTML PDF
