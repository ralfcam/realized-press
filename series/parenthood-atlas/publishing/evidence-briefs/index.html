Deferred Imitation After a Delay
Fourteen-month-olds can reproduce novel multi-step actions on objects after delays of up to one week without prior motor practice; this establishes preverbal recall memory, not neonatal imitation or an accelerated copying curriculum. Infant Contingency Learning
Infants can learn that a self-generated action predicts a sensory consequence in the mobile-conjugate-reinforcement paradigm; this is action–outcome learning under controlled conditions, not adult-like causal theory or a stimulation mandate.		 HIGH-CONTROLLED; LOW-TRANSFER learning, play HTML PDF
Infant Perceptual Narrowing for Speech Contrasts
During the first year, discrimination of native-language phonetic contrasts is maintained or strengthened while sensitivity to some nonnative contrasts declines; this is attunement, not a permanent closing of language learning.		 HIGH-LAB; MODERATE-TIMETABLE; LOW-SEALED-DEADLINE language HTML PDF
Infant Social Referencing Under Uncertainty
Late in the first year, infants can use a familiar adult’s emotional signal to regulate approach or withdrawal in an ambiguous situation; referencing is not an attachment classification.		 HIGH-LAB; LOW-ATTACHMENT-VERDICT social, regulation HTML PDF
Infant Statistical Speech Segmentation
Eight-month-olds can use transitional probabilities in a brief artificial-speech stream to distinguish word-like units from part-words; this is laboratory segmentation, not proof that statistics alone explain natural language.		 HIGH-LAB; LOW-TRANSFER language, learning HTML PDF
Still-Face Disruption of Expected Social Contingency
When a familiar adult suddenly becomes unresponsive in face-to-face interaction, infants typically protest, look away, and try to re-engage; this is a laboratory signature of expected contingency, not a diagnosis of attachment or co-regulation failure.		 HIGH-LAB; LOW-ATTACHMENT-VERDICT emotion, regulation HTML PDF
Looking Time Is a Measurement, Not a Window into Thought
Infant looking-time methods reliably index perceptual discrimination, novelty, and attentional salience under controlled conditions; looking duration is not a transparent readout of rich conceptual knowledge, belief, or innate theories.		 HIGH-METHOD; LOW-RICH-INTERPRETATION cognition HTML PDF
Neonatal Cardiopulmonary Transition
Birth reorganizes gas exchange and circulation after placental separation; shunt closure is functional, not instant destiny, and a difficult transition is not a personality forecast.		 HIGH-SEQUENCE; MODERATE-TIMING birth, regulation HTML PDF
Periconceptional Folic Acid and Neural-Tube Defects
Randomized and systematic-review evidence shows periconceptional folic acid substantially reduces neural-tube-defect risk; this does not generalize to all anomalies or to intelligence.		 HIGH-NTD; LOW-OTHER-ANOMALIES-COGNITION pregnancy, nutrition HTML PDF
